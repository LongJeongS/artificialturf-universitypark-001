Best Grade of Artificial Grass for a University Park Yard

Published 2026-11-09 · University Park Turf Co.

Short answer

For a University Park backyard, the best grade of artificial grass is often a polyethylene turf with mixed blade colors, a brown thatch layer, a shaped blade, a pile around 1 1/2 inches and a heavier face weight. One distributor's buying guide ties higher face weight to greater density and durability. Get the backing and infill named on the quote.

You can look for the best grade of artificial grass for a long time and end up holding nothing but spec sheets. We would pick the grade that still looks like a lawn from the kitchen window in year eight. Once you know what pile height, face weight, yarn shape, backing and infill describe, a spec sheet gets a lot easier to use. The cheapest roll is usually one flat bright green, which is a big part of why it looks fake once it is down. The rest of what we do is on our artificial turf installation University Park TX homepage.

What do pile height and face weight tell you?

Pile height is the blade length, measured from the backing to the tip. One product line in a surfaces distributor's buying guide runs from a little over half an inch to about 1 3/4 inches, and other products may fall outside that. Taller turf tends to look fuller on a quiet lawn, and the same guide says shorter piles are typically used for sports and activity surfaces. For a backyard that gets real use, something around 1 1/2 inches is often a reasonable place to start. If you live in Highland Park, the town's code sets blade length on residential installations at 1 1/2 to 2 1/2 inches, requires blades and backing of lead-free polyethylene or a vegetable-based material, and requires colors that mimic natural turf. Our post on Highland Park's artificial turf rules has the rest of the section, so check a product against it and confirm with the town before you choose.

Face weight is the weight of the yarn alone in a square yard, before the backing. The same guide says a higher face weight relates to greater density and durability. Read the two numbers together. A tall pile with a light face weight is a thin crowd of long blades, and it can mat down faster than you would like. A moderate pile with a heavier face weight tends to keep standing.

The total weight on the sheet adds the backing, and infill too if it is included. It says more about how stable the roll is than how the surface looks.

How much do yarn shape and color matter?

Pick up a cheap sample and a better one and roll a blade between your fingers. The cheap blade is often flat and smooth, like a ribbon. A better one usually has a spine or fold down its length, shaped like a C, a W, an S or a diamond in cross section. That shape generally helps a blade spring back after a foot comes off it, and it can cut down on shine.

Real St. Augustine has olive, lime and a little tan in it, with brown at the base. Better turf often copies that with two or three blade colors and a curled thatch layer underneath in brown or khaki. In the soft light under big trees, that thatch does most of the work of looking real, and a flat emerald blade with no thatch can look like a putting mat from across the patio.

Yarn material is the last check. Polyethylene is a common choice for lawns, and stiffer yarns tend to show up where a ball needs to roll true. Our putting greens University Park TX page covers a short dense putting surface with longer fringe around it, and feeling the two side by side shows why one yard can need two products.

What should be underneath the blades?

Flip the sample over. The backing is generally two layers: a fabric the blades are tufted through, and a coating that locks the tufts in place. That coating is usually latex or polyurethane. Polyurethane generally costs more and is often sold as holding tufts tighter and handling heat and moisture better, so ask for the backing type by name on the quote. Look for drainage holes punched evenly through the backing, or a fully permeable backing, because a backyard under trees can stay wet after a storm.

Infill is the loose material brushed in between the blades. It helps hold them upright and weighs the turf down. Washed silica sand is a common baseline. Coated or rounded mineral infills generally cost more and tend to stay cleaner, and for dog areas an infill made to help control odor can be worth paying for. For anyone weighing rubber infill, the EPA's federal research on recycled tire crumb covers playing fields and playgrounds, describes likely exposure as limited, and says its reports are not a risk assessment.

Whatever product you are shown, ask how the seams will be joined, how the edges will be hidden and how the perimeter will be secured. It helps to have those answers before you compare prices.

How do you read a sample in your own yard?

Judge turf in the place it will live, at the time of day you will see it most. Ask for at least three samples and lay them where the lawn will go.

Then step on each one in shoes and watch how fast the blades stand back up. Pour a glass of water on it to get a rough feel for how it drains. Hold it up to the sky and look at how evenly the blades cover the backing, and don't count the drainage holes against it. Run your palm against the grain and feel whether the blades fold or push back.

Ask the installer to write the product name, pile height, face weight, backing type and infill on the quote. Turf grade, size and base prep set the price of a job, and our Dallas artificial turf cost guide walks through all three. A written quote with the spec named is how you compare two bids that look the same on the total line.

Frequently Asked Questions

What pile height is best for a backyard lawn in University Park?

For a lawn that gets walked and played on, around 1 1/2 inches is often a reasonable place to start. Longer piles tend to look fuller and may mat down faster, and shorter piles are typically used for activity surfaces. One product line we looked at runs from a little over half an inch to about 1 3/4 inches.

What face weight should artificial grass have?

Face weight is ounces of yarn per square yard before the backing, and a higher number generally means denser, more durable turf. Read it alongside pile height, since a tall, light product may mat quickly and a moderate height with a heavy face weight tends to keep standing. A backyard often suits the heavier end of a line.

Why does cheap artificial grass look fake?

The giveaways are usually flat, shiny blades that reflect light in one sheet, a single bright green color with no thatch underneath, and a low face weight that lets blades lean and show the backing. Mixed tones, a shaped blade and a brown thatch layer tend to help with all three, so look for those.

Is polyurethane backing worth it over latex?

For the sunny and wet parts of a yard, it can be worth asking about. Polyurethane is often sold as holding tufts tighter and handling heat and moisture better than latex, and it usually costs more. Have your installer explain which backing a product uses and why, and compare the options on a written quote.
